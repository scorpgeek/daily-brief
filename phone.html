10One Long-Form ReadObjection

Get a Tech List to Tick

Abhay Karandikar (Member, S&T, NITI Aayog) and Aditya Sinha (Associate Director, FAST India)

The argument

India's outward push rests on Aatmanirbhar, read as independence rather than self-centredness. Three semiconductor plants and the National Quantum Mission are operational, PLIs cover more than a dozen sectors, an indigenous 4G/5G stack has been deployed, and RDIF carries ₹1 lakh crore. The authors argue the instruments are sufficient and the missing piece is judgement: which technologies India cannot afford to depend on others for. Every other major economy publishes such a list.

The claim I'm attacking

That India needs an evidence-based list of critical technologies, and that publishing one would improve the targeting of public money and private capital.

The joint

The piece conflates two different questions and then admits it in a subordinate clause. Criticality is a claim about consequence — how badly the country is hurt if supply stops. Winnability is a claim about probability — whether domestic capability is achievable at all. The authors write "Being critical is not the same as being winnable" and treat that as sufficient, then produce a list asked to serve both purposes at once. Nothing in between says who makes the second judgement, on what evidence, or what happens when something critical is not winnable.

The argument

A list is a prioritisation mechanism, and prioritisation is achieved by exclusion. The alists work precisely because they are short and contestable. The list described here does the opposite: assembled by adding every technology where dependence is uncomfortable, then annotated. On that construction it cannot prioritise, because nothing is excluded. And the annotation step has no owner. Winnability is a five-year, multi-billion-dollar, cross-ministerial judgement about industrial capability — it cannot sit unassigned at the bottom of an op-ed. The deeper problem is that a list works only where an enforcing institution exists. The US export-control lists bite because Commerce has statutory authority and the industry litigates against them. India currently has neither.

What I would concede

The authors are right on three counts that matter. Capability compounds and lead times run to years, so reactive policy is structurally too late. Supply denial is genuinely now statecraft, and the 2022 chip cut, the 2023 gallium and germanium response and the April 2025 rare-earth controls show a denial cascade moving in weeks. And private returns to deep research do fall short of social returns, which is a real reason public money has to lead. The Kautilya frame at the close is apt: a store is defined as much by what it leaves out.

What would change my mind

A published list with measurable redirection — if RDIF issuance patterns visibly shift toward named technologies, or ANRF reserves a portfolio slice against the list and reports against it annually, then the mechanism is working and my objection fails. A second thing would change my mind: evidence that an existing institution has been assigned enforcing authority over the list.

How this differs

The authors argue from inside the framing — they accept that a list is the right instrument and show what it would look like. My objection accepts that and shows the instrument cannot do the job as specified without an enforcer and an exclusion rule, both unassigned. They ask what the list should say; I ask who decides what it omits.

14Traps in My English
"Please note that the SLA has been breached"→ "We've breached the SLA by six hours"
Please note that plus a passive buries the news and hides the magnitude. The most damaging one in customer service because it forces the reader to extract the fact.
"As per the discussion held on yesterday"→ "As we discussed yesterday"
As per and discussion held are both redundant. Say it in four words.
"The work will be completed by tomorrow"→ "The work will be complete tomorrow"
It will be complete, not be being completed. Wrong tense reads as a plan, not a commitment.
"There are many challenges which we are facing in the current scenario"→ "We're facing three problems: X, Y and Z"
Naming them is the entire point. Vague quantity makes the listener do your work.
"We are writing to inform you that your ticket has been resolved"→ "Your ticket is resolved"
Seven words doing the work of one.
15Speak

Prompt

Your team's last three monthly client reports contained the same error, and the client has flagged it. You've been asked to explain it to your team. Speak.

Model answer

Right, let me take this straight. Three reports, same error, and the client caught it before we did. That's on me for not checking.

Here's what happened. We built a template in March and nobody reviewed it after the second month. It wasn't a one-off — it was a trajectory, and it went unnoticed because each report looked plausible. That's the part I want to be honest about: we had a check that passed on bad data.

What I've done. The figures in those three reports are provisional until we re-run them against the source. I've told the client that in writing rather than waiting for them to ask. And we're adding a second reviewer on anything that goes out monthly.

To mitigate it happening again, I'm taking the template off the shared drive so it can't be copied forward. That sounds small. It's the actual fix.

Anyone see a gap in that, I'd rather hear now.
16Quiz5 questions

1. LTM says 60% of its portfolio has completed AI deflation. Deflation here means:

The same output delivered with fewer people. The term is borrowed from economics; here effort falls while output holds.

2. Which sentence uses provisional correctly?

Provisional means not yet final and subject to change. The others stretch it to mean casual or temporary.

3. Register. You must tell a client their SLA was breached. Which is right?

States the fact, the size and the remedy. A hides the magnitude in a passive; B adds ceremony without information.

4. Ring-fence the vendor's access means:

To isolate and protect. Common in risk and compliance contexts.

5. An official says the government should manage the rupee's trajectory. Trajectory means:

The direction and shape of movement over time, as distinct from a single level. That distinction is what the official is making.
