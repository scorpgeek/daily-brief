Kolkata pandal stampede

printed pp.1, 10

Background
A Durga Puja pandal in north Kolkata filled with people sheltering from heavy rain on Saturday evening. Organisers then made an announcement asking people to leave, saying the structure might collapse at any moment; private security staff engaged by the pandal also blew whistles. The police have registered a suo motu case under Section 105 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita — culpable homicide not amounting to murder.
Affected
Nineteen dead, of whom 13 were female including two minors. The youngest were a seven-year-old boy from Bihar, a 13-year-old from Agarpara in North 24 Parganas and a 14-year-old from Purba Bardhaman. About 25 were injured, 14 of them shifted to Apollo Hospitals. Seven of the dead came from North 24 Parganas. The State has announced ₹25 lakh to the next of kin of each deceased.
Next
A magisterial inquiry by an additional collector is under way and is meant to establish at which level the lapses occurred. The Chief Minister has said the State will write to the Calcutta High Court seeking a special investigation team headed by a sitting judge, with Kolkata police kept out. Fire and police officials have since inspected pandals and urged organisers to increase lighting.
Watch
Whether the SIT is constituted, and what its first finding is on how the surge began. The editorial's charge is specific and testable — that only temporary home guards were posted at the venue, and that effective deployment of experienced police may have avoided the tragedy.
Scale
Nineteen dead at a structure the paper notes has generally been accident-free despite very large crowds. The failure was in the crowd's movement, not in the pandal.

10One Long-Form ReadObjection

Developing minds in a digital world, a changing India

Derick H. Lindquist

The argument

Children and teenagers now move constantly between chats, video, social media, games and AI platforms, and with about 65 crore Indians under 25 the paper treats this as a population-level developmental question. The author's claim is that technology is not the problem; constant exposure during maturation is. The prefrontal cortex, which supports attention, working memory, planning and impulse control, keeps maturing into early adulthood, and dense repetitive digital input may strengthen some circuits while leaving others underdeveloped. On attention specifically, the mechanism is not the brain but the reward loop: short videos, autoplay, infinite scroll and algorithmic recommendation produce variable reinforcement, which makes ordinary experience — a face-to-face conversation, practising a skill, sitting with one's thoughts — feel comparatively unrewarding. On effort, the author draws a line between cognitive offloading, which is harmless and long-established, and substitution, which is not: photographing the car park saves you a memory, but an essay written by a model removes the effort through which the skill is built. His recommendations follow: device-free times and places, protected study periods, unstructured time, oral examinations and supervised writing, and greater scrutiny and disclosure duties on platforms serving young people.

The claim I'm attacking

That protecting productive struggle — device-free periods, oral examinations, supervised writing — is the right response to artificial intelligence displacing mental effort in young people.

The joint

The weakest joint is evidential, and the piece half-concedes it. Every link from screen time to brain structure in the article is correlational, and the author says so in terms. But he then moves straight to recommending classroom and parenting practice as though the causal claim held. The gap is between 'screens correlate with altered brain connectivity' and 'remove screens from study hours and children will reason better'. Nothing in the paper closes it, and the recommendations are written as though it did.

The argument

Take the paper's own evidence at face value and the case for its recommendation weakens rather than strengthens. The sharpest mechanism in the piece is not the prefrontal cortex at all — it is that the cortex matures into early adulthood while 65 crore Indians are under 25. If the vulnerable window is developmental and the exposures are developmental, then the interventions with the best claim to matter are those that change the environment for everyone at once. What the paper actually recommends is the small, voluntary, high-compliance-requiring kind: device-free times and places, protected study periods, unstructured time. A recommendation that works only if every parent and every teacher executes it consistently is not a policy. It is an appeal, and when it fails it will look like a moral failing on the part of families rather than a design failure on the part of platforms. The paper locates the incentive problem precisely — the corporations designing these tools have a strong financial interest in keeping young people engaged — and then declines to name a single mechanism by which a parent enforcing a bedtime changes that incentive. Note also what the recommendations omit: the paper does not propose a single obligation for the platforms themselves beyond asking policymakers to require better evidence of harm. Its regulatory paragraph is a list of things somebody else ought to do. The honest version of the argument is therefore narrower than the one made. What the evidence supports is a serious, well-motivated hypothesis about developmental exposure, and what that warrants is independent research and enforceable disclosure duties on the platforms that profit from the exposure. It does not yet warrant a rewritten pedagogy delivered through parental goodwill. A Director reading this should notice that the piece proves more about regulation than about parenting, and says so considerably less often than it should.

What I would concede

The distinction between scaffolding and substitution survives the objection intact. If AI is doing the thinking, the harm is real whether or not it can yet be demonstrated in a cohort, and the argument from what is being replaced — the struggle of being wrong and revising — does not depend on the neuroscience at all. I would also concede the boredom point without reservation: a piece arguing that unfilled time has value, against an economy that fills it, is making a claim that has been true in every generation and is not thereby false.

What would change my mind

If a cohort of children raised under enforced device-free periods and AI-restricted schoolwork showed no measurable difference from matched peers in sustained attention, working memory, or persistence on problems with no available answer, the argument collapses entirely — and with it the recommendation to protect productive struggle. The paper concedes it cannot run that study; the objection is that it should have said plainly that until someone does, the recommendation is a hypothesis wearing the clothes of a policy.

How this differs

The source argues we should change how children are taught; the objection argues that the same evidence points at platform design and enforceable disclosure, and that a recommendation resting on adult compliance is not a policy at all. Where the piece is optimistic about voluntary family practice, the objection is sceptical — and says the piece has chosen the weaker of the two arguments actually available to it.

09Opinion: 2-Minute Read

Developing minds in a digital world, a changing India

Derick H. Lindquist, Dean, Jindal School of Psychology and Counselling, O.P. Jindal Global University (printed p.6)

Claim
Constant exposure to powerful digital tools while the brain is still maturing — generative AI especially, which replaces mental effort rather than assisting it — may shape how young people think, feel and act across a lifetime, and the remedy is to protect productive struggle rather than remove screens.
Support
The strongest support is the distinction between scaffolding and substitution. AI can generate ideas, organise arguments and summarise material; what it cannot manufacture is the struggle of finding the words, being wrong and revising. The recommendations follow from that distinction: device-free times and places, protected study periods, unstructured time, oral examinations, supervised writing, applied problem-solving.
Leaves out
It concedes the evidence is correlational and then argues as though it were causal. It never prices its own recommendation — a school that reinstates supervised writing and oral exams produces fewer graduates per term, and neither cost nor capacity appears anywhere. The corporations it names as having a financial interest in keeping children engaged are never named or held to anything.
Sit with
If the exposure happens during a developmental window and the fix is protected offline time, why is the piece addressed to parents and schools rather than to the platforms whose design creates the exposure in the first place?
Steal
It opens with a scene every reader recognises — a teenager moving between chats, videos, social media and games — and earns the right to generalise from it. The turn comes when it separates assistance from substitution using a physical example: photographing the car park so you need not remember where you parked. That analogy does more argumentative work than a page of neuroscience, because the reader has already accepted cognitive offloading before being told it is a problem.

Revamp functioning of Parliament

Atharva Deshmukh and Mehul Chhabra, policy researchers and LAMP Fellows (printed p.7)

Claim
Delimitation will bring MPs closer to their constituents but no more powerful, because representation operates in two layers and the bill only addresses one of them.
Support
The numbers do the arguing. Lok Sabha strength is proposed to rise from 550 to 850 while Question Hour permits 20 starred and 230 unstarred questions a day, ran 60% of schedule in the 17th Lok Sabha, and ran 1% — nine minutes in total — in the 2026 Monsoon Session. Constituencies range from 57,953 voters in Lakshadweep to 37.8 lakh in Malkajgiri.
Leaves out
It treats a fixed timetable as a given when the timetable is itself a political choice made by the body it criticises. And it offers no mechanism for the reform it most needs: protecting Zero Hour and Question Hour requires either a rules change the Speaker may not have power to make or the constitutional amendment it has already shown is missing.
Sit with
If the same House that cannot make time for its own members would have to legislate a minimum sitting-days clause, what is the realistic path to it?
Steal
It opens by conceding the obvious case for the reform before attacking it — yes, more MPs is right; no, it is not enough — which buys the right to be severe afterwards. The phrase doing most of the work is 'functional capacity', an unglossed noun that quietly converts a headcount argument into a capacity argument.

Analysing female labour of the North East States and UTs

Chandrasekar K. (printed p.7)

Claim
The headline national rise in female labour force participation conceals a spread of more than ten percentage points between states, and pace of change alone cannot tell you which places are genuinely catching up.
Support
It separates the aggregate from the distribution, then does what most columns skip: it refuses to stop at pace. Arunachal Pradesh's +8.28 points a year looks remarkable until you see it began below the national baseline; Nagaland and Meghalaya began above it and still gained. Speed and starting position are different variables, and the piece plots both.
Leaves out
It never says why. Every State is treated as a dot on a scatter rather than a place with a labour market, a childcare regime or a safety environment, so the reader gets a very good map and no theory of what produces the pattern. Delhi's decline is reported without a word of explanation.
Sit with
What is Delhi doing that Arunachal Pradesh, starting lower, is not?
Steal
The method is the argument. It builds four charts in sequence — pace, spread, then pace plotted against starting level — so that by the fourth the reader is doing the author's reasoning rather than receiving it. Naming the fourth chart's purpose in advance tells you what to look for before you find it.

14Traps in My English
We are having a discussion with the vendor regarding the escalation matrix.→ We are discussing the escalation matrix with the vendor.
A discussion is countable — you have one. 'Having a discussion regarding' reaches for a noun where the verb already exists, which is the most common tic in Indian corporate English.
Please revert back.→ Please revert.
The 'back' adds nothing; revert already means reply. It reads as a tic rather than a meaning precisely because it has been normal for so long that nobody questions it.
The attrition rate is decreased by 4%.→ Attrition fell by 4%.
Rates fall, they are not decreased. 'Decreased by' also hides the direction, leaving a reader to work out whether minus four is good news.
We are having a delay from the client side.→ There's a delay on the client's side.
A delay comes from the client rather than being handed to you by them. 'From' reads as a small accusation you do not need to make on a client call.
I will escalate this to the higher management.→ I'll escalate this to my manager.
In a BPO the layer above you is a manager. A Director hearing 'higher management' from their own team has to ask who, and the answer is never flattering.
15Speak

Prompt

Your vendor has missed the same SLA three weeks running, your operations manager is asking why you have not escalated, and the client is on the line tomorrow. What do you say to your manager?

Model answer

I want to be straight about where this stands. We have had three consecutive misses on the same SLA, and the vendor's own root-cause report still points at a process on their side, so escalating internally would just move the problem somewhere it can still fail. The salient issue is that my recovery plan is contingent on data I do not have yet. So here is what I need from you: authority to put the last thirty days of ticket-level data in front of the vendor's account lead by Wednesday, and cover for me on the client call. Every day this stays left hanging eats into the goodwill we have with that account. If you would rather I hold the line and go in without the data, say so and I will — but I would rather facilitate this myself than escalate something that still has no remedy attached.
16Quiz5 questions

1. A supplier promises 'prompt and stringent action' after a failure, and nothing happens for three weeks. What is 'stringent' promising?

'Stringent' covers severity, not speed — 'prompt' already carries that, and the supplier used both words in one sentence. Option a is 'prompt' restated, which is the trap: two adjectives, two different jobs. Read the phrase as speed plus teeth, never speed alone.

2. A report says 'the incentive is contingent on hitting the SLA'. In an operations review, what is that telling you?

'Contingent on X' means 'dependent on X' and nothing more. Option b smuggles in a comparison the sentence never makes, which is how this word gets over-read in a board pack. Option c is a claim about sequence, which is not what 'contingent' means at all.

3. Which of these is safe to put in a client email?

A discussion is countable, so 'we are discussing' is correct; 'having a discussion' and 'a discussion regarding' reach for a noun where the verb already exists. Option c is the more expensive error — the past tense implies the meeting has happened, and if it has not, you have just misinformed the client.

4. You tell the team the five-minute handover 'eats into' the shrinkage target. What does that mean?

'Eat into' is gradual and partial — it consumes a share, it does not destroy. Option b overstates it to ruin, and option c inverts the meaning entirely, since shrinkage here is the loss you are trying to reduce, not a count that falls.

5. In a shift huddle, which sentence is wrong if you mean the backlog will grow because there is less time?

'Timeline' names the plan rather than the time available, and 'we will carry more over' states a consequence with no mechanism attached — which is exactly the sentence that gets challenged with 'why?'. Options a and c both name the compression and the consequence, which is what makes a sentence actionable on a floor.

17Skip List
The daily Sudoku, the bank fixed-deposit and home-loan rate tables on printed p.13, the Hyderabad race card, and the film-music blurbs — none of it is news. Watch for paid content dressed as features: the INBRIEF boxes are edited summaries rather than advertising, but the festival and shopping items sit close to that line, and the front-page subscription QR code is a promotion that reads as layout.
